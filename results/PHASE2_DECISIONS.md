# Phase 2 decisions

Read this before starting model development. Every number below is measured; the generating artefacts are named after each claim. Produced by `src/14_phase2_decisions.py`.

## 1. Which scaffold variant, and does the 0.761 need correcting?

**Keep the chemically-aware Bemis-Murcko scaffold; the 0.761 stands and needs no correction.** `src/03_splits.py` already uses `MurckoScaffold.GetScaffoldForMol` (src/tox21lib/splits.py:28 scaffold_smiles() calls MurckoScaffold.GetScaffoldForMol(mol)), which preserves atom identity and bond order - not the generic framework. The generic variant was the thing that could have inflated our leakage numbers, and it is strictly coarser: 1285 unique scaffolds against 2156 (ratio 0.596), largest group 1501 (`C1CCCCC1`) against 1260 (`c1ccccc1`), and 895 singletons against 1658. 4060 of 6143 molecules would change group, always by merging distinct cores, which would make the split harsher rather than fixing an error. The reported scaffold-split macro ROC-AUC of 0.7611 ± 0.0227 is therefore correct as published (`results/scaffold_audit.md`). The generic framework is worth reporting only as an optional harsher stress test.

## 2. Does chemical-space overlap support the shared-encoder experiment?

**No - the measurement says negligible overlap, and this is the finding that should reshape the plan.** The median nearest-neighbour Tanimoto from each of the 6143 Tox21 molecules to any Davis molecule is **0.1667** (p25 0.1333, p75 0.2000), below the 0.25 negligible-overlap threshold; only 0.47% of Tox21 molecules have any Davis neighbour above 0.4 and 0.36% above 0.6. The reverse direction looks better (median 0.3553) only because 19 of Davis's 68 drugs are literally present in Tox21 and score 1.0. All seven physicochemical distributions differ with KS p < 1e-3, most sharply aromatic ring count and ring count (`results/chemical_space_overlap.md`, `figures/chemspace_pca.png`). Consequence, stated as the spec requires: the shared-encoder experiment will likely show no gain for distributional reasons. Options, in order of cost: (a) reframe the research question from "shared encoder beats single-task" to "what happens when multi-task learning spans disjoint chemical spaces" - the negative result is then the contribution and the numbers above are its evidence; (b) add bridging data, i.e. molecules that are both drug-like and tox-labelled (ToxCast, or hERG, which already shares 135 InChIKeys with Tox21); (c) keep the claim but pre-register it as a null hypothesis test. Do not report a shared-encoder gain as expected.

## 3. Which binding dataset carries the main results?

**KIBA carries the main results; Davis is the secondary comparison.** KIBA has 2030 drugs x 229 proteins over 117193 measured pairs (density 0.2521); Davis has 68 drugs x 379 proteins over 25772 pairs - a complete matrix (density 1.00) in which 18343 pairs (71.2%) sit at the censored Kd = 10000 nM ceiling, so its median pKd is the ceiling value itself. On usable split settings the test-set sizes are decisive: Davis `cold_drug` tests only 7 distinct drugs, and the instability shows directly in the seed spread - Pearson 0.4530 ± 0.1897, Spearman 0.3815 ± 0.2324 - versus KIBA `cold_drug` with 203 test drugs and Pearson 0.7324 ± 0.0305. Usable: KIBA random, cold_drug and cold_target, plus Davis random (Pearson 0.6773 ± 0.0093). Report Davis cold_drug only with its seed spread attached, never as a headline. Note that `cold_target` is weaker on both datasets (KIBA Pearson 0.5917, Davis 0.5809) and tests only 23 and 38 proteins respectively - the effective sample for protein generalisation is the protein count, not the pair count (`results/binding_baseline.md`).

## 4. Does hERG corroborate the Tox21 leakage finding?

**No - it diverges, and the divergence is explained rather than mysterious.** On hERG (588 molecules, 581 labelled, positive rate 0.6867) the random-split macro ROC-AUC is 0.8577 ± 0.0639 and the scaffold-split is 0.8665 ± 0.0630, i.e. a drop of -0.0088 against Tox21's +0.0713. Two measured reasons, both in `results/herg_baseline.md`: hERG's test folds hold only 58-59 molecules, so the seed-to-seed spread (0.0639) is roughly seven times the random-versus-scaffold gap - the gap is inside the noise; and hERG is intrinsically more scaffold-diverse (0.658 unique scaffolds per molecule against Tox21's 0.351), so a random split already separates test chemistry from training. The measured test-to-train nearest-neighbour similarity falls by only 0.0897 on hERG when switching to the scaffold split, against 0.1450 on Tox21. Conclusion for the report: the 0.071 Tox21 drop is real but should be described as dataset-specific - a consequence of Tox21's scaffold redundancy (the benzene group alone holds 1260 molecules) - not as a general law of scaffold splitting. Do not generalise the leakage claim beyond Tox21 without a third dataset with comparably sized folds.

## 5. Is the docking benchmark big and diverse enough for Vina vs DiffDock?

**Yes on size and diversity; with one caveat on conformational balance.** 20 structures were selected from 271 queried EGFR entries with no filter relaxed (no relaxation needed), each with a cleaned receptor, a crystallographic-coordinate ligand SDF with CCD-derived bond orders, and a JSON record; all 251 rejections are logged with reasons. Chemical diversity is real, not twenty erlotinib analogues: over 190 ligand pairs the Tanimoto similarity is median 0.159 (min 0.085, mean 0.193, max 0.831), with only 4 pairs above 0.7 and erlotinib absent from the set entirely; all 20 ligands have distinct InChIKeys. 20 structures gives a usable paired comparison - at this size a paired Wilcoxon test over per-structure RMSD can detect a consistent method difference, which two structures never could. The caveat: ranking by resolution produced 3 active and 17 inactive conformations (salt-bridge distances 2.69-3.09 A versus 9.20-15.58 A), so the set is dominated by the inactive state while our screening receptor 1M17 is active. For pose-accuracy redocking this is acceptable because each ligand is redocked into its own receptor, but any claim about Vina/DiffDock performance on the active ATP-site conformation specifically rests on 3 structures - so either report results stratified by conformation, or extend the set with more active-state entries from the 74 that passed every filter but fell outside the 20-structure limit (`results/docking_benchmark_set.md`).

---

## Net effect on the proposal

One claim weakens and one strengthens. The shared-encoder premise is not supported by the data (median nearest-neighbour Tanimoto 0.1667) and must be reframed or bridged before six weeks go into it; the binding half of the project is in better shape than assumed, because KIBA supports a real cold-drug evaluation (203 test drugs) where Davis never could (7). The Tox21 scaffold-split number needs no correction, and the docking benchmark is ready to use.
