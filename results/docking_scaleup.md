# Redocking across unrelated targets

Generated by `src/41_docking_scaleup.py`. AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exhaustiveness 8, 9 modes, `--cpu 1`, project seed 0. Identical settings to the Phase 4 EGFR run.

## 1. Success rate, one target versus many

| set | complexes | top-pose within 2 A | best-of-9 within 2 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 4 EGFR (superseded) | 20 | 41.0% | 76.0% |
| astex | 77 | 62.3% | 84.4% |
| posebusters | 370 | 50.8% | 77.3% |

Pooled over the 447 complexes that produced a pose: 52.8% top-pose and 78.5% best-of-9.

**What the denominator excludes.** 66 of 513 complexes produced no pose, and the split between the two reasons matters for reading the rate above: 61 were **deliberately excluded** because the residues Meeko could not parameterise line the binding site, and 5 failed receptor preparation outright. The first group is not a docking failure - those receptors could have been docked after deleting the offending residues, but the resulting pocket would not be the one the crystal ligand binds, so their RMSD is not comparable with the rest. Counting them as misses would understate the rate; counting them as successes after mutilating the pocket would overstate it. They are listed in the CSV with the residues named.

## 3. Scope

This is self-docking: the search box comes from the crystal ligand of the same structure being redocked, so these rates are an upper bound on what the tool achieves on a target whose binding site is not already known. The ligand geometry is rebuilt by ETKDG rather than taken from the crystal, so the pose itself is not handed to the search.
