# Toxicity reliability (Phase 4, tasks T1-T3)

Three checks the toxicity half had never had: an external sanity set, a calibration audit of the probabilities the demo will show, and a confidence interval that accounts for split choice as well as test-set sampling. Generated by `src/28_tox_reliability.py`.

## T1 - literature reference compounds

Structures are fetched from PubChem by name (so none is typed from memory); the *expectation* for each is literature/textbook pharmacology and is **not** measured by us. A compound already in the model's training fold is not an external check and is flagged as such.

Cyclohexanol is the worst of them: a simple alcohol predicted active on SR-MMP - and at 7 heavy atoms it sits **below the pipeline's own 10-heavy-atom training filter**, so it is an out-of-domain query, not merely a wrong answer. The forest answers confidently anyway, which is the cleanest possible argument for an applicability-domain gate (task S1) before anything is served to a user.

Caveat on the headline: only 5 of the 14 compounds are genuinely external, so the 40% external match rate is n=5 and cannot support a conclusion in either direction. It is a smoke test, reported because the toxicity half previously had no external check at all.

## T2 - calibration

| split | ECE | Brier | mean predicted | observed rate | ECE after isotonic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| random | 0.0602 | 0.0611 | 0.1444 | 0.0842 | 0.0252 |
| scaffold | 0.0645 | 0.0727 | 0.1571 | 0.0926 | 0.0253 |

The forests are **over-confident in the positive direction**: mean predicted probability 0.157 against an observed positive rate of 0.093 on the scaffold split - a ~1.7x over-prediction. This is `class_weight='balanced'` miscalibrating **by construction**: re-weighting the minority class shifts the decision threshold to help ranking, and the resulting scores are no longer estimates of P(active). **Verdict: the raw probabilities are not usable as percentages in a demo UI.** Fitting isotonic regression on the validation fold (never on test) reduces mean per-fit ECE from 0.0722 to 0.0253 on the scaffold split; ship that, or show ranks instead of probabilities.

Selective prediction - rejecting the least confident predictions, confidence being distance from 0.5:

| split | rejected | kept | macro ROC-AUC | accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| random | 10% | 26988 | 0.8098 | 0.9559 |
| random | 20% | 23989 | 0.7926 | 0.9684 |
| random | 50% | 14993 | 0.7687 | 0.9814 |
| scaffold | 10% | 24962 | 0.7253 | 0.9429 |
| scaffold | 20% | 22188 | 0.7050 | 0.9564 |
| scaffold | 50% | 13868 | 0.6699 | 0.9758 |

**Read the last two columns before believing the accuracy column.** Rejecting on distance-from-0.5 in a 3-18% positive setting throws away positives: at 50% rejection on the scaffold split the kept pool is 2.7% positive while the rejected pool is 15.8% positive. Accuracy rises because what remains is easy negatives, and macro ROC-AUC *falls* on the same pool. So this is **not** a usable confidence measure as constructed: it abstains on exactly the molecules a toxicity screen exists to find. A real abstention rule would have to be built on distance to the training chemistry, not on distance from 0.5.

## T3 - an interval that includes split choice

The published bootstrap interval resamples test molecules only. Per-seed macro differences give the split-choice component: 0.0542, 0.0898, 0.0358, 0.0835, 0.0932 (sd 0.0251, sem 0.0112).

| interval | lower | upper | excludes zero |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bootstrap only (test-set resampling) | 0.0510 | 0.0919 | yes |
| **combined with split-seed spread** | **0.0413** | **0.1013** | **yes** |

Method: per-seed macro differences give the split-choice component; the bootstrap percentile interval gives the test-set sampling component as an implied sigma = (hi - lo) / (2 * 1.96); the two are treated as independent so the variances add, and the combined interval is mean +/- 1.96 * sqrt(boot_sd^2 + seed_sem^2).

The scaffold-split penalty of 0.0713 survives the wider interval, so the Phase 2 finding stands under both sources of variance.
